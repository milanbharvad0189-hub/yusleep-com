How To Read A Supplement Label

Key Takeaways
  • Serving Size vs. Servings Per Container
  • Named Amounts vs. Proprietary Blends
  • "Other Ingredients"

Supplement labels pack a lot of information into a small space. Knowing how to read one properly makes it much easier to compare products honestly — including YU SLEEP.

Serving Size vs. Servings Per Container

The serving size tells you how much to take at once (2 capsules for YU SLEEP); servings per container tells you how long one bottle actually lasts (60 capsules per bottle). Multiply the two together before assuming a bottle will last a full month.

Named Amounts vs. Proprietary Blends

Some labels list the exact milligram amount next to every ingredient; others group several ingredients into one "blend" with a single combined total. On YU SLEEP's own label, each ingredient amount is listed individually on the label. Neither approach alone tells you whether a product works, but named amounts make it easier to compare against published research.

"Other Ingredients"

Below the main ingredient panel, most labels list "other ingredients" — things like capsule shells, binders or preservatives. These are not the active ingredients but are worth a glance if you have known sensitivities.

Reading YU SLEEP's Label Specifically

YU SLEEP lists 9 named ingredients. Melatonin is included for its role in sleep-wake cycle regulator. L-Theanine is included for its role in calming amino acid. GABA is included for its role in nervous system relaxant. Valerian Root is included for its role in traditional sleep herb.

Cost Per Serving, Not Just Cost Per Bottle

A bigger bottle is not automatically a better deal. Divide the total price by the number of servings per bottle to get a true cost-per-day figure, then compare that across package sizes before deciding which size of YU SLEEP to order.

Quick Checklist

  • Check the serving size, not just the bottle size.
  • Look for named amounts where possible.
  • Scan "other ingredients" for anything you need to avoid.
  • Compare cost per serving, not just cost per bottle.
